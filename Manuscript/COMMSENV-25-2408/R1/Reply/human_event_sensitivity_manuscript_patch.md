# Regional human-event sensitivity manuscript patch

## Methods insertion

We used archaeological summed probability densities (SPDs) as the primary
proxy for human activity because they are assembled independently of the
pollen responses analysed here. SPDs nevertheless have uneven archaeological
coverage and may underestimate human presence where dated material is sparse.
We therefore used the pollen-derived expert chronology of major land-use
events as a complementary sensitivity test. The event chronology is not
independent of the palaeoecological record and is too coarse to represent local
human presence, land-use intensity, or ecological impact; it was not treated
as the primary human predictor.

We compared three descriptions of human influence: SPD, Combined (SPD plus the
event categories applicable to each continent), and Events only. Categories
were selected separately for each continent and the corresponding regional
reference state was excluded. Asia used `fi`, `fc`, and `ei` with `bi` as the
reference; Europe used `fi`, `fc`, `ec`, and `cc` with `bi` as the reference;
North America used `fc` and `es` with `bi` as the reference; Central and South
America used `weak` and `strong` with `no_impact` as the reference; and Oceania
used `weak`, `medium`, and `strong` with `no_impact` as the reference. Climate
predictors, vegetation-response variables, age bins, and temporal and spatial
controls were held constant. All 1,270 pollen records were screened by experts
using the predefined regional event criteria. At least one qualifying event
was detected in 1,024 records. The remaining 246 records were also assessed,
but no human-impact indication meeting the event criteria was detected, so
they remained in the corresponding regional reference state. Their
classification does not imply an absence of people or human influence. The
all records remained candidates for analysis, after which the same
response-availability and model-estimability requirements were applied to all
three proxy formulations. Event-only trajectories were additionally shown for
the younger interval not covered by SPD.

## Results insertion

For the principal presentation, negative hierarchical fractions were set to
zero and the remaining positive human, climate, and temporal components were
renormalised to sum to one. Adding the regional event chronology to SPD shifted
this human--climate importance balance toward human predictors. Among 148
matched pollen records with a defined Combined comparison, Combined minus SPD
had a median change of 0.346 (interquartile range 0.047--0.736). Among 146
records with a defined Events-only comparison, Events only minus SPD had a
median change of 0.158 (interquartile range -0.098--0.647). These values
describe redistribution among positive hierarchical contributions, not a
change in the absolute amount of variation explained.

Across 61 matched continent-by-age estimates, Combined minus SPD changed the
zero-truncated human share by a median of 0.031 (interquartile range
0.015--0.068) using all data. Events only minus SPD was centred near zero
(median -0.012; interquartile range -0.057--0.022). The temporal and spatial conclusions were
therefore retained when the regional event chronology was added to, or
substituted for, SPD. Complete continent-specific results, including ranking
and model-estimability changes, are reported in the supplementary evidence
tables.

## Discussion insertion

The archaeological SPD and the pollen-derived event chronology provide
complementary rather than equivalent evidence of past human activity. SPD is
independent of the pollen response but is affected by uneven archaeological
sampling and is unavailable here after 2 ka BP. The event chronology captures
broad, region-specific land-use transitions and extends into this younger
period, but its pollen basis introduces circularity and its classes cannot
represent the full intensity or ecological effects of human activity. We therefore retain SPD as the primary proxy and treat agreement with events
as sensitivity evidence, not independent confirmation.
