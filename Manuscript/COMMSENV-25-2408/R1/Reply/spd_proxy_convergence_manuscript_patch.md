# SPD–external proxy convergence manuscript patch

This patch is deliberately separated from the live manuscript for author
review. Its numerical values were generated by the registered target pipeline
and are also read directly by `response_spd_proxy_convergence.qmd` from the
exported target products.

## Methods

We evaluated convergence between the archaeological radiocarbon summed
probability distribution (SPD) and two external human-impact reconstructions:
the KK10 annual fraction of each 5-arc-minute grid cell under land use and HYDE
3.2 population per grid cell. For each pollen-record location, raster cells
were included when their centres fell within the archaeological neighbourhood
used for that record (250 km where a usable SPD was available, otherwise 500
km). KK10 was latitude-area-weighted and HYDE population was summed over the
included cells. HYDE values were linearly
interpolated without extrapolation to the common 500-year ages between 8 and
2 ka BP. Following Gordon et al. (2025), SPD and HYDE population were
square-root transformed, whereas KK10 remained on its fractional scale.
Observations were assigned to deciles defined by transformed SPD, retaining
tied cut points, and each proxy was represented by its median and empirical
2.5th–97.5th percentile range within each bin. We calculated Kendall's tau
between SPD-bin medians and the corresponding KK10 and HYDE medians overall and
by continental region. Regional correlations were reported only when at least
eight matched bins each contained at least five pollen records and three ages.
Uncertainty was estimated from 2,000 bootstrap samples of complete
pollen-record histories (seed 342). Sensitivity analyses used first
differences, 5 and 20 SPD-defined bins, strict 250 and 500 km neighbourhoods,
and point rather than buffer extraction.

## Results

The common comparison contained 1,181 records across 13 500-year ages. Overall,
SPD-bin medians were strongly positively associated with KK10 land-use fraction
(Kendall's tau = 0.87, 95% cluster-bootstrap interval 0.64--0.91) and HYDE 3.2
population (tau = 0.69, interval 0.56--0.78). Regional estimates were positive
for both proxies in Asia and Europe; estimates were more heterogeneous in Latin
America, North America, and Oceania. Level correlations remained positive with
5 or 20 bins, strict 250 or 500 km radii, and point extraction. First-difference
correlations were weak (tau = 0.07 for both proxies), indicating that convergence
primarily reflects broad gradients rather than short-term co-change. These
results indicate convergence among independently constructed proxies, but do
not imply that any proxy is unbiased or represents ground truth.

## Discussion

Agreement between SPD, KK10, and HYDE would strengthen the interpretation that
the broad SPD gradient contains information about human activity beyond the
research and preservation processes affecting radiocarbon dates. Conversely,
weak or heterogeneous agreement would limit that claim and should be reported
as such. KK10 and HYDE are model-based reconstructions with their own input
assumptions and coarse spatiotemporal support, so convergence cannot validate
local demographic histories or remove the need to discuss archaeological
sampling bias. The analysis is therefore framed as triangulation among
imperfect proxies rather than validation against a truth dataset.
